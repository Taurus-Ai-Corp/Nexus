## THE HARD TRUTHS

### 1. You've Already Done the Work Without a Contract

This is the single biggest mistake. You delivered $15,000+ worth of work — 45 commits, 18 days, 223 assets — with no signed service agreement, no milestone payments, and no formal scope document. The client has a live, production-quality website right now. You have nothing on paper.

**What could go wrong:**
- Client says "we thought the $2,000 covered everything including Phase 2 and 3"
- Client shares the site with another developer who clones it
- Client's governing board says "we never authorized this expense"
- Your friend (who brought the deal) gets caught in the middle

**Fix RIGHT NOW:**
- Send the Service Agreement (Document 02) TODAY
- Send the Project Completion Certificate (Document 03) for Phase 1 sign-off
- Send the Invoice (Document 01) with the $4,000 figure
- Get signatures BEFORE you present to the board

### 2. The $2,000 → $4,000 Jump Needs a Story

Your memory says the agreed fee was $2,000. You're now invoicing $4,000. This needs to be handled carefully:

**Honest framing:**
> "The original estimate of $2,000 was based on a standard landing page. During development, the scope expanded significantly — you requested 20+ client-annotated edits, investment tiers, governance board section, brochure download system, and comprehensive mobile optimization. The actual deliverable is 4x what was originally discussed. I'm invoicing $4,000, which is still 60-85% below what any agency would charge for this scope."

**If they push back:** You can offer $3,000 as a "goodwill" number. Below $3,000, you're literally working for less than an Indian freelancer's rate for this scope.

### 3. The Friend Dynamic Is a Liability

Your friend brought this client. That means:
- You can't be as firm on payment terms (you feel social pressure)
- The client may expect "friend pricing" forever
- If there's a dispute, your friendship is at risk
- Your friend may feel responsible if the client doesn't pay

**Protect yourself:**
- Keep all communication professional and in writing
- The Service Agreement creates a boundary between friendship and business
- Your friend's role should be acknowledged but not entangled in the contract
- If your friend has equity in your company, disclose this to the client

### 4. You're a One-Person Operation Billing as an Agency

This is not inherently a problem — many successful studios are one-person with AI tooling. But be honest about it:

**What to say:** "NeoVibe by Taurus AI leverages AI-augmented development, which means I deliver agency-quality work at a fraction of the cost and timeline."

**What NOT to say:** "We have a team of designers and developers." They will eventually find out, and credibility is everything with a first client.

### 5. The Board Presentation Is Your Make-or-Break Moment

9 board members. A bishop. A chairman. Directors. This is an audience that:
- Has never hired a web developer before (probably)
- Doesn't understand what they're paying for
- Will ask "why does a website cost this much?"
- May have a nephew/cousin who "knows computers"

**Prepare for these questions:**
- "My nephew can make a website on Wix for free" → "A Wix site won't rank on Google, won't capture leads, won't handle the investment tier system, and won't represent the premium brand you've built."
- "Why can't we just use Facebook?" → "Facebook doesn't capture investor leads with tier selection, doesn't show up when someone Googles 'Mater Maria Homes', and you don't own your audience there."
- "What happens if you get hit by a bus?" → "The source code is on GitHub, deployed on Vercel, and documented. Any developer can maintain it. I'll also provide a handoff document."

### 6. You Have No Maintenance Revenue Model Yet

The website is live. It needs:
- Domain management (matermariahomes.com)
- Hosting (currently Vercel free tier — fine for now)
- Content updates (they WILL want changes)
- Security patches
- Analytics monitoring

**This is your recurring revenue opportunity.** The $400/month retainer in your Service Agreement is where the real money is over time. $400 x 12 months = $4,800/year — more than the initial project fee.

### 7. UAE Free Zone Tax Advantages — Use Them

Your IFZA FZCO gives you:
- 0% corporate tax on first AED 375K profit
- 0% VAT on exported digital services (this client is in India)
- No TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) obligation — UAE-India DTAA Article 7
- International wire transfers with no forex restrictions

**BUT:** The client may try to deduct TDS (Indian tax) when paying you. Educate them: "As a UAE-registered entity, this payment is exempt from Indian TDS under the UAE-India Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. I can provide a Tax Residency Certificate if needed."

### 8. Questions You Should Be Asking (But Aren't)

1. **"Do you have a signed agreement with the Diocese for this project?"** — If the governing board hasn't formally authorized the website expense, your invoice may get stuck in bureaucratic approval.

2. **"Who specifically authorizes payments?"** — Is it Rajeev (Chairman)? Thomas (MD)? The Bishop? The Diocese finance office? You need to know whose desk your invoice lands on.

3. **"What's your fiscal year and payment cycle?"** — Indian organizations (especially church-affiliated) often have specific payment processing windows.

4. **"Do you need a proforma invoice before the actual invoice?"** — Many Indian organizations require a proforma first for budget approval.

5. **"Will you need a Tax Residency Certificate from me?"** — Get this from IFZA proactively. It prevents TDS issues.

6. **"Are there any other stakeholders who need to approve the website before it goes live on the custom domain?"** — Don't assume the board presentation = final approval.

7. **"What's the content plan for Phase 2?"** — Don't start Phase 2 without written confirmation of who provides photos, copy, and floor plans. Content is ALWAYS the bottleneck.

8. **"Have you budgeted for ongoing website costs?"** — Plant the seed for the retainer now, not after launch.
