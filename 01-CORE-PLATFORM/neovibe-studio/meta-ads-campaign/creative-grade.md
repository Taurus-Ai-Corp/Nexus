# NeoVibe — Creative Grade Report

## Executive Verdict

3 of 5 variants ship as-is, 2 need iteration, 0 retire. The campaign mostly nails peer-voice but stumbles on one generic CTA and one variant that drifts into SaaS-marketing speak.

## Variant 1 — Stop Stitching 7 Tools

```
Hook: Stop stitching 7 tools.
CTA: Apply Now
```

Strongest line: *"Slack for the brand kit nobody can find."* This is the single most peer-true line in the entire campaign. Every freelancer working with a 5-person studio has lost the brand kit in Slack. Naming it earns trust faster than any feature claim.

Weakest: *"Your brand kit lives where you design."* This is the only sentence in the variant that reads like landing-page marketing — vague subject ("your brand kit"), vague verb ("lives"), vague where. Everything around it is specific; this line is not.

Rewrite: *"The brand kit sits in the same canvas as the file you're designing — no more 'where did Anu put the logos' Slack search."*

Score: 29/30 — SHIP. The campaign's lead variant. Highest score across both campaigns.

## Variant 2 — AI That Actually Ships Code

```
Hook: AI that actually ships code.
CTA: Get Access
```

Strongest line: *"For freelancers and 2-5 person studios who'd rather build than babysit prompts."* "Babysit prompts" is the exact phrase a working designer uses about Midjourney/v0/Lovable in 2026. It is self-aware, audience-true, and gates the audience by attitude rather than feature.

Weakest: *"NeoVibe's playground exports production-ready Next.js + Tailwind components — typed, themeable, ready to ship."* This is a feature triplet ("typed, themeable, ready to ship") in the SaaS-marketing rhythm the voice brief explicitly warned about. Designers smell this.

Rewrite: *"NeoVibe's playground exports a typed Next.js + Tailwind component you can paste into a client repo without rewriting the props."*

Score: 28/30 — SHIP. Pair with V1 in the A/B for week 1.

## Variant 3 — Curated Workspace, Not Free-for-All

```
Hook: Curated. Not crowded.
CTA: Apply Now
```

Strongest line: *"Every 'AI design platform' you tried last year is now overrun with crypto-coin landing pages and dropshipping templates."* This is a real, specific, slightly-rude observation that a designer would actually make. The quoted "AI design platform" doing skeptical work is correct.

Weakest: *"We approve designers we'd hire ourselves."* This is the line that breaks the variant. It is founder-marketing-speak — the kind of sentence designers paste into group chats with an eye-roll caption. It also makes a claim the brand cannot actually back without disclosing the hiring bar.

Rewrite: *"We approve based on portfolio, not signup forms. Roughly 1 in 4 applications gets in."* Specific, falsifiable, doesn't flatter the gatekeeper.

Also weak: *"AI image + video gen, RAG over your past briefs, code export."* Three-feature run-on without verbs. Designers want verbs.

Score: 24/30 — ITERATE. Rewrite the "we'd hire ourselves" line and reverbalize the feature list before it ships. After rewrite this likely lifts to 27.

## Variant 4 — From Brief to Component in Minutes

```
Hook: Brief in. Component out.
CTA: Sign Up
```

Strongest line: *"Send the link. Get paid Tuesday."* This is the campaign's tightest peer line — three monosyllables, end of workflow, tied to money. The voice brief ("designers are romantic about getting paid, not about their work") is executed perfectly here.

Weakest: the CTA. "Sign Up" against a body that has just promised paid-on-Tuesday velocity is a flat ending. The other variants in this campaign use "Apply Now" / "Get Access" — both better fits for the application-only positioning. "Sign Up" implies free open registration, which contradicts every other variant in the set.

Rewrite (CTA only): **Apply Now** (consistent with V1, V3, V5). If a different verb is wanted, **Request Access** matches the 48-hour-approval promise in the body.

Score: 26/30 — SHIP after CTA fix. Strong workflow narrative; storyboard visual is the most concrete in the set.

## Variant 5 — Built by Freelancers for Freelancers

```
Hook: We built it because we were tired.
CTA: Apply Now
```

Strongest line: *"We approve in 48 hours, or tell you why not."* The "or tell you why not" half-clause is the entire voice in seven words. It addresses the rejection anxiety that application-only products usually paper over. This is the line that should be on a billboard.

Weakest: *"No Discord required."* This is the campaign's only line that tries too hard — it is a self-aware quip without a specific referent (Discord-required for what?). It reads as filler rather than observation. It also slightly contradicts the rest of the variant's quiet confidence.

Rewrite: drop the line. Replace with *"Your assets stay in the workspace, not in someone's DMs."* — keeps the same anti-Discord sentiment but ties it to the IP/security concern that V3 raised, building campaign coherence.

Note on the "We built it because we were tired" hook: this is the right register but borders on the well-worn "we built it for ourselves" SaaS founder trope. It survives because the variant immediately gets specific (Kerala-and-Dubai, Figma + ChatGPT + Midjourney + Notion + Drive).

Score: 26/30 — SHIP. Best Malayalam-localization candidate per production notes.

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

What worked: the campaign disciplined itself to name real tools (Figma, ChatGPT, Midjourney, Notion, Slack, Drive, Loom, VSCode, Next.js, Tailwind) instead of generic categories. Every variant has at least one specific concrete time/output number (90 seconds, 48 hours, 200 designers, 7 tools, 2-5 person studios). The "application-only" gate is reframed across variants in three different ways — exclusivity (V1), curation against decay (V3), honesty (V5) — which gives the audience three different entry points to the same positioning. Self-deprecating peer voice ("babysit prompts", "Get paid Tuesday", "we were tired", "or tell you why not") is the campaign's superpower and it is consistent.

What failed: the campaign has a recurring tic of feature-triplets in SaaS-marketing rhythm — *"typed, themeable, ready to ship"* (V2), *"AI image + video gen, RAG over your past briefs, code export"* (V3), *"AI gen baked in. Code export. RAG over your asset library"* (V5). Designers parse these as press-release filler. Each triplet should be replaced with one verb-driven sentence. Second pattern: CTAs are inconsistent across variants (Apply Now / Get Access / Apply Now / Sign Up / Apply Now) — Sign Up in V4 is the outlier and contradicts the application-only positioning. Standardize. Third: the campaign assumes RAG is a known acronym to the audience. Most working freelancers in 2026 know it; some do not. Either define it once across the campaign ("the system remembers your past briefs") or trust V4's correct handling ("the RAG knows your past assets, your brand kit") — never leave it as a bare acronym in a hook line.

The campaign as a whole over-relies on tool-stack enumeration (Figma + ChatGPT + Midjourney + Notion + Drive appears in V1 and V5, near-verbatim). One enumeration is the proof; two starts to feel like the campaign has only one observation.

## Ship Recommendation (best to worst)

1. **V1 Stop Stitching 7 Tools** (29) — sharpest tool-pain identification; fund first, run as 1:1 + 9:16 immediately.
2. **V2 AI That Ships Code** (28) — best wedge against AI-mockup objection; pair with V1 as the rational-A test.
3. **V4 Brief In, Component Out** (26) — strongest workflow narrative; ship after CTA fix to "Apply Now".
4. **V5 Built by Freelancers** (26) — best founder-voice variant; lead candidate for Malayalam localization in Kerala set.
5. **V3 Curated, Not Crowded** (24) — hold from week-1 budget; rewrite "we'd hire ourselves" and reverbalize feature list before ship.
